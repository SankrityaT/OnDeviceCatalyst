# Governance

OnDeviceCatalyst currently uses founder-led, evidence-driven governance.

## Roles

- The founder owns final spec acceptance, releases, repository access, security
  coordination, and the public-private research boundary.
- Maintainers review code, specs, evidence, and community interactions in their
  areas of demonstrated responsibility.
- Contributors may propose, implement, test, benchmark, document, and review
  work without repository write access.

## Decisions

Substantial changes require a ticket, reviewed specification, and founder
approval. Lasting architecture, compatibility, dependency, governance, or
release decisions also require an ADR.

Review feedback is evaluated on correctness, evidence, risks, maintenance cost,
and alignment with the roadmap. Raw vote counts do not decide technical work.

## Maintainers

Maintainer access is earned through sustained technical judgment, constructive
review, reliable follow-through, respect for confidentiality, and adherence to
the project's evidence standards. There is no fixed commit-count threshold.

The governance model may move toward maintainer-group decisions after multiple
active maintainers have established shared context. That change requires its own
approved governance spec and ADR.

## Research boundary

Public contributions do not automatically make someone a collaborator or author
on private research. A human must agree to research access, IP, confidentiality,
and authorship expectations before contributing to the private program.

If a publication substantively relies on a public contributor's novel method,
analysis, or experiment design, credit is handled transparently under the target
venue's policies. Ordinary use of public software is cited as software and does
not automatically create paper authorship.

## Conflicts and recusals

Reviewers should disclose material conflicts of interest. The founder may ask a
maintainer to recuse from a decision involving personal, financial, employment,
or publication conflicts.
